## Is there a working RadiAnt discount code?

No. There is no coupon field to hunt for, no seasonal sale, and no affiliate code that shaves anything off. Medixant sells this software at list price through its own store, and the store page states that it reserves the right to change the offer at any time, which is the closest thing to a promotion policy you'll find there.

Two genuine price breaks do exist, and neither is a checkout code.

The first is a special discount for journal article authors, linked from the store page and handled through a separate request rather than at payment. If you're publishing and citing the software, it's worth applying before you buy.

The second is an early renewal bonus on subscriptions. Renew before your current term expires and you get extra time equal to 20% of the plan you're on. On a monthly plan that's 6 extra days; on a yearly plan it works out to roughly 73 days; on the five-year plan it's a full 365 days. That's a real 20% cut in effective cost, but only if you're already a subscriber and you remember to renew early.

One thing that looks like a discount but isn't: the `cid` parameter on the link in this article is a tracking value, not a promo code. Typing it into a coupon box will do nothing.

Prices are listed in euros and don't include local taxes or customs charges. The store converts to your currency using rates updated daily, so the euro figure stays fixed while your local number drifts. If you're in the EU, expect VAT to land on top at checkout.

The single most important line in that table is buried in the subscription terms. A subscription is a time-based licence: you get the software and all updates for as long as you've prepaid, and when it expires you have to buy a new plan to regain access. It doesn't downgrade to a read-only mode or keep working without updates. The program stops being usable.

The CD/DVD licence works the other way. It's permanent, never expires, and comes with 12 months of free updates. After those 12 months you can keep using the version you have indefinitely, or pay €80 a year to keep receiving updates. Maintenance renewals start from the date the previous period ended, no matter when you actually pay, so buying late doesn't extend anything.

Pricing is per machine, not per person. Subscriptions are priced per PC or per client terminal, and the CD/DVD licence is priced per recorder, meaning the machine that writes the discs. There's no floating or shared licence mentioned anywhere on the store page. A five-seat reading room on yearly subscriptions is €195 a year, and that's the honest number to budget.

Volume tiers only exist on the CD/DVD side. Two to five recorders drop to €359 each, six or more to €319 each, so two units cost €718 in total. Subscriptions have no volume tier at all.

## Which RadiAnt plan fits you

**You read studies on a Windows machine most days.** Take the yearly subscription. At €39 it beats twelve months of monthly billing by €21, or about 35%, and it pays for itself against the monthly plan after roughly eight months. Anyone using this as a daily work tool clears that in the first quarter.

**You need it for a short stretch.** A locum week, a backlog of discs from a departing colleague, a single project. Pay €5 for one month and stop. Buying a year to look at twenty scans is the most common way people waste money on this software.

**Your department has several workstations.** Multiply by seat count and budget accordingly, because there is no shared licence. If those machines are offline, can't be tied to a renewing subscription, or need to keep working years from now without anyone remembering to renew, the permanent CD/DVD licence is the only option that survives an expired invoice. It costs ten times a yearly subscription per machine, so make that call deliberately.

**You're on a Mac.** Don't buy. RadiAnt is a Windows application, sold for Windows 7 through 11, and the vendor's position is that running it on macOS means CrossOver, with features missing or unstable, unsupported. If your reading machine is a Mac, this is the wrong purchase regardless of price. 👉 [Compare the plans yourself before deciding](https://lizhi.shop/products/radiant-dicom-viewer?cid=7bbix2xj).

**You only need to convert or de-identify files.** RadiAnt is a viewer with PACS query and retrieve, MPR, 3D volume rendering, PET-CT fusion and DSA. If your actual job is editing DICOM tags or stripping identifiers, you're paying for a reading tool and getting a read-only tag tree. Buy something built for that instead.

## The drawbacks worth knowing before you pay

The Windows-only limitation is the big one, and it's structural rather than a missing feature that might arrive later. A competitor's comparison page describes Mac support as CrossOver-only and explicitly unsupported by the vendor, which matches how the product is sold: per Windows PC.

Subscription expiry catches people out. If you stop paying, you lose access to the software, not just future updates. Anyone who has been burned by a lapsed licence on a deadline day should factor that into the decision, and departments with awkward procurement cycles should look hard at the permanent licence.

The CD/DVD edition is not the full program. RadiAnt's own product page points to a comparison table and states plainly that the disc version doesn't include all the features of the installable version. If you're buying it for the permanence, check that the features you actually use survived the cut.

Export options are narrower than you might expect from a viewer in this price bracket. The same third-party comparison lists JPEG and BMP images plus WMV movies, with no NIfTI export, and notes that DICOM tags are shown read-only, with values settable only through custom anonymisation templates added in version 2024.1. That comparison comes from a competing product, so read it with that in mind, but the feature limits it describes are specific enough to verify on the vendor's own pages.

One more thing to be clear about: the vendor's manual describes RadiAnt as not a medical product, without FDA or CE certification. Whether and how you use it in a clinical workflow is a question for your own institution's rules, not something a pricing article can settle.

Buying through a regional reseller can also cost more than the vendor's own euro price. One Swiss distributor lists the CD/DVD licence at 453.51 USD with 0% VAT, against €399 from the source. Check the currency and the tax treatment before you assume a local dealer is cheaper.

## FAQ

**Does RadiAnt keep working after my subscription expires?**
No. The licence is time-based and access ends with the term. You need a new plan to use the software again. Only the CD/DVD licence is permanent.

**How many computers does one licence cover?**
One. Subscription prices are per PC or client terminal, and the CD/DVD price is per recorder. Multiple machines mean multiple licences.

**Can I use it on a Mac?**
Only through CrossOver, which the vendor doesn't support, and with features missing or unstable. There's no native macOS build.

**Is the CD/DVD licence the same software?**
It's the same viewer with a permanent licence and autorun from disc, but it does not include every feature of the installable version. Check the vendor's comparison table before buying it for the permanence alone.

**Are the listed prices what I'll actually pay?**
Not necessarily. They're in euros, exclude local taxes and customs, and get converted at daily rates. The tax-inclusive total on the reseller's checkout page is the real number.
